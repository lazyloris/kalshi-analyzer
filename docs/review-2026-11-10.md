# Independent review of the Kalshi research (2026-11-10)

Reviewer: a fresh Claude session with the repo at `main` (PRs #16 and #17 merged) and the raw history from the GitHub release `backtest-data` (13 MB tar, 2026-11-09 21:09Z). Everything below was recomputed locally from that data; where a number could not be checked it says **unverified**.

Severity: **High** = changes a conclusion or could lose money. **Medium** = changes how much to trust a number. **Low** = cosmetic or small. Confidence is how sure I am of the finding itself.

## Plain-English summary for the owner

The code is honest: I looked for places where a decision could peek at the future and found none that matter, the fees match Kalshi's published formula, and the settlement data is clean. The problem is the method, not the code. Over 400 strategy variants were tried on the same prices, so a result that looks "significant" at t = 3 is roughly what luck alone produces, and most of the winners were in fact tuned on the same months they were then scored on. The headline survivor, buying NO on 1-4c Bitcoin long shots, is real but pays about 1 cent per dollar, has fat-tail risk the data under-shows, and is too small to matter. However, a plain look at the data without any model turned up something better and more consistent: **Bitcoin "above X" favorites priced 75-96c about 3-4 hours before the 5pm close win more often than their price says, in both years and in every quarter, in deep markets.** It still came out of a search (about 60 price-by-time cells per asset), so it is a candidate, not a proven edge. My recommendation: stop building forecast models (weather, volatility), stop the minute and swing work, run the favorite rule as a pre-registered paper book for 60 days with the kill rules written down in advance, and in parallel test a maker version of it from Kalshi's trade feed. Realistic upside if it holds: low hundreds of dollars a month at retail size, which covers Claude. It will not scale far beyond that.

## A. Audit of code and method

### A1. Look-ahead bias, spot by spot

| Spot | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `engine.quote()` (`backtest/engine.py:43-57`) | **Clean** | Kalshi candle `end_period_ts` is the inclusive end of the period and `yes_bid/yes_ask.close` is the quote at that instant (Kalshi API spec, verified). A candle ending at or before the entry time contains nothing from after it. |
| `crypto_events` / `_price_at` (`engine.py:119-164`) | **Clean** | Coinbase candles are keyed by candle END (`data.py:244`) and looked up with `bisect_right(hours, ts) - 1`, so the price at `ts` is the close of the hour ending at or before `ts`. Spot can be up to 3h old (`engine.py:162`), measured below: it is almost always 0h old. |
| `signals._tod_vol`, `spike_fade`, `skip_spiked`, `by_trend`, `volume_move` | **Clean** | All windows end at `e["ts"]`; `_mid_before` uses `quote(..., ts - 3h, max_age_h=2)`. |
| `bayes` settle lag (`bayes.py:112-115`) | **Clean, conservative** | Outcomes enter the posterior 2 days after the event date, later than Kalshi actually settles. |
| `fattail.attach` (`fattail.py:280-292`) | **Clean** | Only h-hour moves that END at or before `ts`. |
| `minute_lag.simulate` (`minute_lag.py:118-122, 145-149`) | **Clean in code, borderline in reality** | Coinbase minute close at `t` and Kalshi quote at `t` are the same instant; in practice you observe one and hit the other some seconds later. | `swing.simulate` (`swing.py:354-394`) | **Clean** | Lock math checked: buy side at `price`, lock when our side's bid is `price + rebound` by buying the other side at `1 - bid`; both legs pay the taker fee; payout is `n` per pair. Correct. |
| `__main__.rolling_pick` (`__main__.py:124-146`) | **Clean per trade, not per menu** | It picks from past trades only (2-day lag), but the *menu* of strategies it picks from was built with full knowledge of the data. It cannot be worse than its inputs' optimism. |

**Measured quote staleness** (`quote()` with `max_age_h=6`): of 8,726 market-quotes used by the late Bitcoin book, 8,715 were 0h old, 8 were 1h, 3 were 3-5h. Main book: 14,679 of 14,689 at 0h. So `max_age_h=6` lets in a handful of stale quotes, not enough to move any number. Kalshi does *not* return a candle for every hour, though: of 294,851 Bitcoin candles, 2,811 gaps of 2h and 1,405 gaps of 5h exist. (Medium severity in principle, Low impact here; confidence high.)

### A2. Settlement and P&L

- **Result parsing** (`data.py:62-65, 68-99`): `result in ("yes","no")` drops voided markets (fine). Cross-check: in all 32,916 KXBTCD markets with a settlement value, `value > floor` agrees with `result == "yes"` 100% of the time. Settlement data is clean. (Confidence high.)
- **Fee formula** (`model.py:47-50`): `ceil(0.07 × C × P × (1−P))` to the next cent per order. This matches Kalshi's published schedule (fee rounded up per **order**, not per contract; rounding over-charges on multi-fill orders are rebated). Verified against three independent write-ups of the Feb/Jul 2026 schedule; Kalshi's own PDF was not reachable from this container, so **the per-order rounding is verified from secondary sources only**. It matters: if rounding were per contract, the late 1-4c fade would fall from +$87 to +$12.
- **Series fee multipliers**: the scanner already stores `fee_type` and `fee_multiplier` from `/series`. In the cached index, KXBTCD, KXETHD, KXBTC, KXHIGHNY, KXGOLDD and KXINXU all have `quadratic`, multiplier 1 (standard 0.07, **no maker fee**). 43 of 179 scanned series are `quadratic_with_maker_fees` (sports game series, e.g. KXNFLGAME). So: the backtest fee is right for every book that was tested. The maker simulation (`signals.py:28, 240`) charges a 0.0175 maker fee on crypto and weather where Kalshi charges **none**; this makes the limit-order results slightly too pessimistic but does not change their sign. (Low; confidence high.)
- **`_trade()` sizing** (`engine.py:232-244`): `floor(10 / ask)` contracts at the ask, fee on the order. Correct. Note it buys NO at `1 − yes_bid`, which is the NO ask. Correct.
- **Bitcoin settlement source**: third-party documentation says Kalshi crypto markets settle on the **CF Benchmarks Bitcoin Real-Time Index averaged over the final 60 seconds**, not a Coinbase print. **Unverified against Kalshi's rules page** (not reachable). The backtest uses the actual settlement value from Kalshi for outcomes, so this only affects the *model's* spot (Coinbase vs the index differ by basis points) and any last-minutes idea in section C.

### A3. Data quality

| Finding | Severity / confidence | Detail and fix |
|---|---|---|
| Strike filter is survivorship against volatile days | **Medium / high** | Candles are fetched only for strikes within 4% of spot **8h before close** (`__main__.py:54-58`), 6% for ETH, and **2% of spot 6h before close** for the minute test (`minute_lag.py:85`). A day where BTC drifts 2%+ (minute test) or 4%+ (hourly) during the window loses exactly the strikes that become relevant, and those are the days when favorites lose and lag signals fire. 4h moves beyond 3σ happen 1.7% of the time here (normal: 0.3%), so the hourly effect is small; for the minute test (2% band) it is not negligible. **Multiple testing.** `dashboard/data/backtest.json` holds 277 strategy/sweep variants; the minute test adds 144, swing 18, scanner another ~40 rules × categories: **over 450 looks at overlapping data**. Bonferroni at 5% for 277 tests needs |t| ≈ 3.5; for 450, ≈ 3.7. They are correlated (nested sweeps), so the effective number is lower, maybe 50-100, which still needs |t| ≈ 3.1-3.3. Against that bar:

- The 1-4c fade (t_day 3.07) is borderline, and its P&L is a rare-event problem: 2 losing days in 328. The 95% upper bound on the losing-day rate is about 1.4%, i.e. ~4.7 losing days per 328, each costing ~$19.5, which would eat almost all of the +$107 the wins made. **The confidence interval at the event level includes zero.**
- Minute lag (t_day 2.5, best of 144) does not clear the bar, and "positive on the older year" is not an out-of-sample check there: the test was designed and run once over all 400 days (`minute_lag.py:182` just splits the same run).
- The in-sample crypto winners (t_day 2.8-4.9 on Mar-Nov) failed the real out-of-sample year, exactly as a selection artefact would.

**A Deflated-Sharpe style sanity check**: with N correlated trials and an in-sample t of 3, the expected maximum t under the null for N = 50 independent-equivalent trials is about 2.9. So t = 3 is what you get for free.

## B. Holes in the thinking

1. **"Fills at the quoted ask for $10" is a safe assumption; "fills for anything bigger" is not.** The candle `ask.close` is the best offer at the end of the hour with unknown size. For the favorite rule below, the median market traded 4,600-58,000 contracts after entry, so tens of contracts will fill; thousands will not without moving the price. The 1-4c fade's "depth at 96-99c NO" is a different matter: you are hitting YES bids of 1-3c, the market's cheapest lottery tickets, and median post-entry volume is 1,800-8,000 contracts across *all* prices. Capacity is a few dollars of profit per day. **Unverified** by order-book data (not reachable).
2. **Polymarket fees are not zero everywhere.** Polymarket added taker fees to its 15-minute crypto markets on 2026-01-06 (up to ~3%, highest at 50c) and funds maker rebates from them; sports and most other markets remain fee-free. The wallet study's copy test (`wallets.py:343`, "no fees assumed") is fine for sports and wrong for the 15-minute crypto bots. The conclusion (copying loses) only gets stronger.
3. **The hourly cron fires every ~6h** (A3). The live paper books are not testing the backtested rules; they are testing "the same rule evaluated at random 6-hourly moments". `MIN_LEAD_HOURS` of 2 for crypto plus a 6h cadence means many days get no look in the window that matters.
4. **The 2026-03-13 split is a fair test for the strategies picked before PR #13 and nothing after it.** Strategies added in PRs #15-#17 (late entries, Bayes, fat-tail, exits, volume, minute lag, swing) were all built and run with the full 600 days visible. Their "older" columns are descriptive, not predictive.
5. **"Positive every quarter" is not evidence for a 99.8%-win strategy.** Any bet that wins 99.8% of the time is positive in every quarter that happens not to contain a loss. The right question is the loss rate's confidence interval (A4).
6. **Rationalisation in the post-mortems.** (a) The log calls the 1-4c fade "a structural favorite-longshot bias"; the full-vol lognormal model's own expected losses on those 868 trades was 10.6, the market's implied 11.9, and the realised count 2. The *model* and the *market* agree; the sample was lucky, or the tail is thinner than both think. The fat-tail measurement in section C says the opposite (tails are fatter), so I lean to lucky. (b) "The calm-2026 theory is wrong" because RMS moves were 1.0-1.4σ: true, but it misreads the data. The RMS is right **and** 82% of 4h moves land inside 1σ (normal: 68%). Both are true because the distribution is fat-tailed, and that is the actual mechanism behind every "vol x0.5" result. (c) `holdout()`'s docstring (A5 #2).
7. **Right conclusion from flawed data**: weather. The weather forecasts are 1-2-day-old NBM runs from Open-Meteo, so "models lose 17-27%" is pessimistic by design. But the *market calibration* tables (no forecast involved) show weather ladders well calibrated at every entry, so the conclusion "no forecast edge worth chasing with free data" stands.
8. **Wrong framing**: the search has been for a *model* that beats the market (temperature, volatility, options, Bayes). The one thing this dataset shows robustly is a *pricing-shape* error (fat tails priced as normal), which no amount of model tuning on 30-day vol will capture reliably. Section C.
9. **Capacity was never asked about.** Even a real 1% edge on $100 a day is $1 a day.
10. **Live and backtest are different programs.** The live `fade()` (`strategy.py`) trades any time ≥ `MIN_LEAD_HOURS` before close; the backtested weather fade trades at fixed clock entries; the live crypto books use `MAX_PRICE 0.90`. Live-vs-backtest comparisons in `reports/backtest.md` compare different rules.

## C. Better patterns: what I ran, and what to run next

### C1. Ran here: the price-level ladder test (new `backtest/ladder.py`, `reports/ladder.md`)

No model. For every settled market with candles, take the quote exactly H hours before close (≤1h old, spread ≤5c), bucket by YES ask, and score two $10 no-model bets held to settlement, with taker fees: buy YES, or buy NO at 1 − bid. Stats are per **event**. Split at 2026-03-13 and by quarter. 60 cells per book, so 1-3 flags per book are expected by luck; what matters is **coherence across adjacent cells and both periods**.

**Bitcoin (600 days, 10,824 markets).** The 4h moves are fat-tailed: 54% land within 0.5σ of the 30-day vol (normal: 38%), 82% within 1σ (68%), 1.7% beyond 3σ (0.3%), RMS 0.98. The market prices the ladder as if moves were normal, so:

Reading: favorites at 75-96c with 2-4h left are underpriced by 4-9 cents on the dollar; mid-range long shots (12-25c) are overpriced; the 96-99c cell (where the team's "late favorite 90-97c" mostly traded) is barely positive because the fee eats it. The effect is **positive in the older year and the newer year, and in 7-8 of 8 quarters** for the 75-88c and 93-96c cells. The pooled 75-99c rule at 3h made 4.1 trades a day, $421 on $10 stakes over 457 days, max drawdown $77, worst day −$58, losses in 39 of 457 events. ETH shows the same shape more weakly (93-96c at 4h: +4.4%, t 3.5, older +5.6%, newer +2.3%; 75-88c at 4h: +4.7%, t 1.3).

Caveats, in order of importance: (1) I found this by scanning ~60 cells per asset, so the flagged cells' t-stats are inflated by selection even though the pattern is coherent across neighbours; (2) 2026-Q4 to date is negative in several cells (−0.6% to −2.1% on pooled rules); (3) fills at the ask for 10 contracts are assumed; (4) the strike-band survivorship (A3) slightly flatters favorites.

Weather: no cell clears the bar except zero-loss 1-4c fades at 3h (+1.1%, 314 trades on lows) and 96-100c lows at 1h (+1.1%). Weather ladders are well calibrated.

### C2. Proposed tests (hypothesis, data, look-ahead control, test, kill criterion)

1. **Pre-registered BTC favorite paper book.** H: YES at 75-96c, 3-4h before 5pm ET, on KXBTCD, returns ≥ +3%/$. Data: live Kalshi `/markets` quotes via Actions (needs a reliable trigger, A3). Look-ahead: none, it is live. Test: 60 days, fixed rule, $10-25 a trade, at most 2 strikes per day (one 75-88c, one 93-96c), log the ask size if `/markets/{t}/orderbook` is readable. **Kill**: return/$ < 0 after 60 days or 120 trades, or 3 losing days in any 10.
2. **Maker version from the trade feed.** H: resting a YES bid 1c below the ask on the same markets gets filled on ≥ 50% of days and earns the spread on top. Data: `GET /markets/trades` (18+ months, `ticker`, `created_time`, `yes_price`, `taker_side`, `count`). Look-ahead control: a resting order at price p placed at time t counts as filled only if a later trade printed at ≤ p for YES (`taker_side == "no"` means someone sold YES into bids). Test: replay fills vs hold-to-settle; compare with taker returns from C1. **Kill**: fill rate < 30% or maker return/$ not above taker's.
3. **Order-flow bursts before the close.** H: a burst of taker-YES volume in the final 2h (size × count z-score ≥ 2 vs the prior 20h) predicts the favorite finishing; a burst of taker-NO predicts the long shot. Data: same trade feed. Control: compute z-score from trades strictly before `t`. Test: condition C1 on burst sign; event-level t. **Kill**: no cell with t_event ≥ 3 that is positive in both halves.
4. **Daily vs hourly Bitcoin consistency (cross-market).** H: KXBTC (hourly) and KXBTCD (daily) imply different distributions for the same 5pm moment; the one with less volume is wrong. Data: hourly candles for both series at the same minute. Control: same timestamp, quotes only. Test: implied P(above k) from the daily ladder vs the hourly market for strike k; trade the cheaper side when they differ by ≥ 5c. **Kill**: fewer than 100 disagreements in 60 days or return/$ ≤ 0.
5. **Settlement-second edge.** H: the last-5-minute quote of a daily market ignores that settlement is a 60-second **average** of the CF index, so a market that sits 0.1% above the strike at 16:59 is priced 95c when the average is already locked at ~99c. Data: minute candles + a free per-second BRTI proxy (none free that I know; Coinbase 1-minute is the closest). Control: price at 16:58 only. Test: YES/NO on markets where |spot − strike| ≥ 0.15% at 16:57-16:58. **Kill**: t_event < 3 or depth under 100 contracts (needs the orderbook endpoint, read-only). Flag: this is where bots live; expect it to be gone.
6. **Kalshi vs Polymarket same event (read-only).** H: Polymarket's BTC daily "above X at 5pm" (if present) differs from Kalshi by ≥ 3c after both fee schedules. Data: Polymarket Gamma/CLOB `prices-history`, Kalshi candles. Control: same minute. Test: count and size of disagreements. **Kill**: fewer than 1 disagreement a day.
7. **Favorite-longshot across categories, done properly.** H: the Bitcoin shape (favorites 75-96c underpriced in the final hours) holds in other price ladders (ETH, gold, S&P when a free source exists) and does **not** hold in sports (the scanner says sports are calibrated). Data: the existing `kalshi_*` caches plus `scan_*` with horizons measured from the **last trade**, not `close_time`. Test: `ladder.py` per series. **Kill**: nothing with t_event ≥ 3 in both halves.
8. **Literature fit**: the favorite-longshot bias is the best-documented prediction-market inefficiency (Thaler & Ziemba 1988; Page & Clemen 2013 on long-horizon prediction markets; Snowberg & Wolfers 2010). It is weakest in liquid short-horizon markets and strongest where retail buys lottery tickets, which matches what the ladder shows (1-4c and 12-25c long shots overpriced, favorites underpriced) and warns that the edge is small and competed.

## D. Paths forward

**Should research continue?** Yes, but narrowly. Stop looking for models. Test the one pricing-shape effect with pre-registered rules, and test the maker side. If both die in 60 days, the honest answer is that Kalshi's cheap edges at hourly resolution are gone, and the codebase is worth more as a data product than as a trader.

**Top 3 tests, in order:** C2.1 (paper book, free, starts now once the trigger is fixed), C2.2 (maker fills from the trade feed, one Actions run), C2.7 (ladder across categories with corrected horizons). C2.5 is tempting but is bot territory.

**If the favorite rule is real.**
- Size: at 75-88c the win rate was 0.90 at an average price of ~0.82; full Kelly is about a third of the bankroll per bet, which is meaningless given the uncertainty. Use **a quarter-Kelly cap of ~8% of the book per event**, and start at $10-25 while the paper book runs. At 93-96c (win 0.988 at ~0.94) a loss costs ~16 wins; cap exposure so that one losing day costs under 5% of the book.
- Tail: fat tails are the *reason* the edge exists and also the risk. The worst 4h moves (1.7% of days beyond 3σ) wipe several weeks of wins. Expect a drawdown of ~20% of the staked amount per year at 4 trades a day; the backtest's max was $77 on $10 stakes.
- Capacity: median post-entry volume of 10k-58k contracts in the 75-96c cells means 50-200 contracts at the ask is plausible, so **$100-300 a day staked at +3-8% is $5-15 a day, roughly $150-400 a month**. That covers Claude. It will not become a salary; beyond that size you are the market maker's counterparty on the wrong side.
- A cautious live paper book: one rule, two cells (75-88c and 93-96c), entries at exactly 4h and 3h before close, no other books changed, kill criteria from C2.1 written into the book's config comment before the first trade.

**Better uses of the codebase without a trading edge.**
- The ladder/calibration tables are something Kalshi traders do not have: "what does X cents at H hours really win, per series". A free weekly calibration report (GitHub Pages or the existing Vercel dashboard) is the cheapest way to find out whether anyone cares; if 100+ people subscribe to an email, a $9-19/month tier for daily tables and alerts is the test. Comparable tools exist (fair-value pages, fee calculators), so the moat is the per-series settled-outcome history, which the repo already keeps.
- Market size: Kalshi reports millions of weekly contracts; the addressable audience for paid analytics is in the low thousands. Realistic: dozens of subscribers, not hundreds.
- First step: publish `reports/ladder.md` as a page with a "get this weekly" email box (Resend is already set up), and post it once where Kalshi traders read.

**What I would stop.** Weather forecast models (the market beats free forecasts; keep only the ladder table). Volatility-model variants (tod, regime, options, fat-tail model: all are indirect ways of saying "fat tails", and the price-level rule says it directly). The minute-lag and swing lines (closed or borderline, and 2% strike bands make the minute data unfit for the question). The Polymarket wallet study (answered). Adding more paper books on 6-hourly cron before fixing the trigger.
